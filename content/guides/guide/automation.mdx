Build one complete path for the hay before adding more branches. Start with material supply, connect the processing equipment and power it, then watch where the output goes. A short working line gives you a clearer next purchase than a collection of disconnected machines.

## Follow the material

![A conveyor carries hay from beside the pile to the stall marked SELL HAY.](/images/find-the-needle/screenshot-1.webp)

In this official screenshot, the belt leads toward the sale point. Follow your own route from its input to its destination when checking a line.

Use this checklist while laying out a line:

1. **Supply:** decide how hay will reach the first machine.
2. **Connection:** follow the belt route from that supply to the machine that needs it.
3. **Power:** check the machine's connection and the status shown in the game.
4. **Output:** leave space for the next processing step and check that material continues moving.

Change one part at a time when something stops. This makes it easier to tell whether your last adjustment helped.

## Buy for the bottleneck

If your setup spends time waiting for hay, inspect the supply side before buying another processor. If hay reaches a machine but does not move beyond it, look at that machine and its output route first. If one section works while the next does not, start at the connection between them.

These are planning checks, not guaranteed diagnoses. Use the machine information in your current build to confirm what it needs.

## Choose what to produce

The official store describes production beyond loose hay, including bales, paper, pulp, pellets and eco bricks. Treat each new product as a possible production chain: check its available machinery, connections and space requirements before spending your budget.

Leave expansion space around a working setup. You can then add a branch without having to untangle the route you already understand.
